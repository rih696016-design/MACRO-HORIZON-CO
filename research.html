Investment Opportunities Explained

  • 1. US Large Cap Equity

    Large-cap equities represent established, financially stable American companies. Investing in this sector provides exposure to the core of the US economy. While generally less volatile than small-caps, they are still subject to market corrections.

  • 2. Apple and Tesla (Individual Stocks)

    Investing in specific companies like Apple or Tesla offers the potential for high growth, but comes with idiosyncratic risk. A single company's earnings report, product delay, or leadership change can cause significant stock price fluctuation.

  • 3. International Equity

    Diversifying outside the US protects against domestic economic downturns. International equities include developed markets (Europe, Japan) and emerging markets (India, Brazil). These carry additional risks such as currency fluctuation and geopolitical instability.

  • 4. Cash & Equivalents

    Cash and cash equivalents (like Treasury bills or money market funds) are crucial for liquidity. They provide a safety net for emergencies and short-term goals, preserving capital even if they historically offer lower returns than equities.

  • 5. Investment Grade Credit

    These are bonds issued by highly rated corporations or governments. They provide regular interest income and are generally lower risk than stocks, making them a core component of capital preservation strategies.

  • 6. Sustainable Infrastructure

    Investing in infrastructure (energy grids, utilities, transportation) offers inflation-linked cash flows. The shift towards sustainable development creates long-term growth potential in renewable energy and green utilities.

  • 7. Private Credit Fund IV

    Private credit involves lending directly to middle-market businesses. It can offer high yields but comes with significant liquidity risk (your money is locked up), credit risk (the borrower might default), and complex fee structures.
